What you get

Tools that tell you what happened to your lines.

Drift check: see every line that changed

A stencil is only useful if it still is your design. The drift check compares the stencil with the lines found in your original and paints the difference.

  • Red marks a line that was lost.
  • Blue marks ink that was added.
  • The numbers show the share kept, lost and added.

Here sensitivity is set low, so the fine hatching was dropped. The check shows exactly where.

Skin preview: judge it on a real tone

Lines that read well on white can disappear on skin. Preview the same stencil on six skin tones before you print.

  • Lines blend into the tone the way a transfer would.
  • Pick a heavier line weight if they look faint.

The same stencil on six tones, drawn live.

True size, in millimetres

Set the width on skin and the tool shows the exact size and the pixels you need at 300 dpi, with a ruler along the image. The stencil goes onto the skin at the size you planned.

  • Right-click Save image today. Direct print and PDF export are planned.

Questions

Before you try it.

Is it really free?

Yes, the tool in the studio is free and has no sign-up. Paid plans are planned for the features still being built, such as AI cleanup, true-size print export and studio accounts. Nothing is charged today.

Is this AI?

Not yet. Today it is a classic image filter: it finds lines by edges and brightness, removes specks and sets the line weight. That keeps it fast, private and predictable on clean drawings. AI cleanup for photos is planned and will sit behind the drift check.

Do you keep my designs?

No. The free tool runs in your browser and does not upload your images. Closing the page discards them.

Can I upload any tattoo?

Only designs you made or have the right to use. We remove material when a rights holder asks.

Which files work?

JPG, PNG and WebP. Large images are scaled down to keep the tool fast. A flat, well-lit photo of a drawing works well.

How do I print a stencil?

Right-click the stencil and save the image, then print it at the size you set. Thermal stencil printers print onto thermal transfer paper. Inkjet printers need stencil ink and inkjet transfer paper. Direct print and PDF export are planned.

What about a full sleeve or a big piece?

Work motif by motif. Smaller parts keep their detail much better than one huge image.

Does it work on a tablet or phone?

Yes, in the browser. A phone app may follow.

Why does it drop my fine hatching?

Lower sensitivity and higher speck removal drop fine lines on purpose. Raise sensitivity, lower speck removal, and check the drift overlay to see what was lost.
