Make a tattoo stencil at home: practical methods

Last updated: November 26, 2026

The short answer

To make a tattoo stencil at home, start with a clean line drawing at the right size. Trace it onto tracing paper, or print it on regular paper and transfer it by hand. Use these methods for practice on paper or with temporary methods. Real tattooing belongs with a trained artist.

Before you make a tattoo stencil at home

You can make a tattoo stencil at home with a printer, some paper and a few basic supplies. A stencil is a line drawing of a design, used as a guide so the artwork lands in the right place and at the right size.

This guide is for tattoo artists who need a backup method, for students practising stencil work, and for people who want a design on paper or a temporary mark. It is not a guide to tattooing yourself or anyone else.

Safety note: real tattooing must be done by a trained artist in a licensed studio, with sterile equipment and proper aftercare. Making a stencil and practising on paper or with temporary methods is a different thing from putting ink in skin. Please do not try to tattoo at home.

What you need

  • A clean line drawing of the design, on paper or as a digital file.
  • A ruler or measuring tape in millimetres.
  • Tracing paper or thin paper.
  • A sharp pencil or fine pen.
  • Carbon or transfer paper, if you are tracing by hand.
  • A regular printer, black ink and plain paper.
  • Masking tape to hold the paper in place.
  • A light source, such as a window or lightbox, to help you trace.

If you work as an artist, use the materials your studio already uses for skin. The list above is for stencil practice on paper and temporary methods.

Method 1: tracing paper and carbon

This is the old-fashioned method and it still works. It needs no electronics.

  1. Place your drawing on a flat surface and lay tracing paper over it.
  2. Trace the main lines with a sharp pencil, pressing evenly.
  3. Skip shading and fill. A stencil needs outlines and key details only.
  4. Place carbon or transfer paper under the tracing, with the carbon side facing your target surface.
  5. Tape the layers together so nothing shifts.
  6. Go over the lines again with a pen to transfer them.

The result is a copy of the lines on the new surface. For practice, that surface is usually paper or a practice material. For other transfer options, see our guide on transfer methods.

Method 2: print on regular paper and transfer

A regular printer can print the stencil lines on plain paper. You can then carry the lines onto a practice surface by hand.

  1. Print the line drawing at 100 percent with no scaling.
  2. Check the size with a ruler. The printed width should match the width you chose.
  3. Place the printout over your practice surface and trace the lines with a pen so they transfer through carbon or transfer paper.
  4. Lift the paper and look at the result. Correct any missed lines with a pencil.

Remember that a stencil is a mirror image of the final design when it is transferred from the back of the paper. Check the direction before you start, especially with text.

The cleaner the line file, the easier every other step becomes. A good file has thin, solid black lines on a white background, no grey haze and no stray dots.

Get the size right

A stencil in the wrong size wastes everything else you did. Decide the size in millimetres first, and make sure the file and the printer both respect it.

  • Export the stencil at 300 dpi.
  • Print at 100 percent. Turn off any option like fit to page or shrink to fit.
  • Measure the printout with a ruler and compare it to your target.
  • If it is off, check the print scale and print again.

Our guide on true-size printing explains the sums in more detail, and the tattoo size chart shows typical sizes for each body part.

Tips for a cleaner result

  • Use fewer, cleaner lines. Too much detail turns into a smudge when you transfer it.
  • Keep a copy of the original and work from the copy.
  • Use a light touch and sharp tools.
  • Test on scrap paper first before using your final sheet.
  • Keep your printer ink dark. Pale lines are hard to follow.
  • Label each sheet with the size and the date, so you know which version you made.

If a stencil is for a real client, the artist who will do the work should approve it. They know their technique and what the skin will allow.

Questions

Can I make a tattoo stencil without a thermal printer?

Yes. You can trace the design on tracing paper, use carbon or transfer paper, or print the lines on a regular printer and transfer them by hand.

What paper do I use for a homemade tattoo stencil?

Thin tracing paper works well for hand tracing, and plain printer paper works for a printed stencil. Choose paper that stays flat and does not tear.

How do I make sure the stencil is the right size?

Set the width in millimetres, export at 300 dpi and print at 100 percent. Then measure the printout with a ruler.

Is it safe to tattoo myself using a stencil?

No. Real tattooing should be done by a trained artist in a licensed studio. A stencil is for practice on paper, for temporary methods or for use by professionals.

Why does my stencil come out blurry?

The source file is probably too small or too soft. Start from a clean, high-resolution line file and print at 300 dpi.
