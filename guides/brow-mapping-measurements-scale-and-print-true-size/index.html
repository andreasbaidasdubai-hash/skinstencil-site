Brow mapping measurements: how to scale a client photo and print true size

Last updated: November 25, 2026

The short answer

A face photo has no real-world scale, so brow mapping measurements need one known length. Enter the client's pupil to pupil distance in millimetres, or place a standard 85.6 mm card as a reference, and the tool converts pixels to millimetres. Then print at 100 percent and check the ruler bar on the printout.

Why a photo has no scale

A photo is a grid of pixels. The same face can fill 800 pixels or 4000, so the pixel distance between two points says nothing about millimetres until you tell the tool one real length. Everything else follows from that one number: each pixel is worth a fixed number of millimetres, and every measurement is a pixel distance multiplied by it.

Option 1: the pupil to pupil distance

Measure the client's pupil to pupil distance with a ruler, looking straight ahead, and type it in. The tool finds the two pupil centres in the photo and works out:

millimetres per pixel = pupil distance in mm ÷ pupil distance in pixels

The tool starts at 63 mm, a typical adult value. Adults vary, so a measured value for your client is more reliable than the default. If you enter 66 instead of 63, every measurement grows by about 5 percent.

Option 2: a card as a reference

A standard bank or ID card is 85.6 mm wide. Hold one flat against the forehead, in the same plane as the face, and photograph it with the client. In the tool, switch to the card option and drag the two points to the ends of the card's long edge. The tool divides 85.6 mm by the pixel distance between them.

Keep the card flat and square to the camera. A card held closer to the lens than the face, or at an angle, gives the wrong scale.

What each measurement means

The tool measures along the face, using the line between the pupils as the horizontal, so a slightly tilted head does not skew the numbers. Values are in millimetres unless stated.

MeasurementWhat it is
Start to end widthThe distance from the start S to the end E, measured along the face's horizontal.
Length S to A to EThe path length of the brow guide through the start, the arch and the end.

How accurate can it be

The numbers are only as good as the photo and the scale. Three things matter most:

  • A straight-on photo. The tool warns when the eye line is tilted by more than 5 degrees or the face looks turned.
  • A correct scale. A wrong pupil distance, or a card in a different plane from the face, scales every number wrongly.
  • Enough resolution. A face that is small in the photo leaves only a few pixels per millimetre.

The tool has not yet been validated against measurements on real clients. Treat the numbers as a drawing aid and check the guide on the client.

Printing at true size

The print option sets the page to the real width of the guide in millimetres and adds a ruler bar.

  1. Set the scale. Enter the pupil distance or place the card.
  2. Print the guide. Use the print option. The guide is printed at its width in millimetres.
  3. Print at 100 percent. In the print dialog choose actual size. Turn off fit to page and shrink to fit.
  4. Check the ruler bar. Lay a real ruler along the bar on the printout. If it is not the length it says, a scaling option is on. Fix it and reprint.

A PNG download works the same way when it carries its 300 dpi size, which a day pass or Pro stores in the file. At 300 dpi a width in millimetres needs this many pixels:

Free downloads carry a watermark and are limited in size, so set the print width yourself in the print dialog, or use the print option, which always prints at the true width.

Questions

Is 63 mm right for every client?

No. It is a typical adult value used as a starting point. Adults differ, so measure your client's pupil distance and enter it for a more reliable scale.

Can I use a card instead of the pupil distance?

Yes. Hold a standard 85.6 mm wide card flat against the forehead, in the same plane as the face, then drag the two reference points to the ends of its long edge.

Why does the tool warn about head tilt?

A tilted head changes the angles and distances in a flat photo. The tool follows the eye line, but a straight-on photo gives more reliable lines. It warns when the tilt is more than 5 degrees.

Does the printout come out at true size?

Yes, when printed at 100 percent. The print option sets the page width in millimetres and adds a ruler bar so you can check the scale with a real ruler.
