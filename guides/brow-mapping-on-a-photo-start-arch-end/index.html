How to do brow mapping on a photo: start, arch and end points

Last updated: November 25, 2026

The short answer

To do brow mapping on a photo, load a straight-on client photo and draw three lines from the side of the nose: straight up for the brow start, through the outer edge of the iris for the arch, and through the outer corner of the eye for the end. Keep the start and the end at the same height.

What brow mapping is

Brow mapping is the step before any brow work: marking where a brow should start, where its highest point sits and where it should end. It gives the artist a reference drawn from the client's own face instead of a guess by eye.

The classic method uses a straight edge or pencil held against the face. The same three lines can be drawn on a photo, which makes them easy to adjust, measure and print. They are standard drawing guides, not rules. The artist decides every point, and the final shape is a matter of judgement and of what the client wants.

The three lines from the nostril

All three lines start at the side of the nose, at the outer edge of the nostril (the ala). Each one is drawn up the face and ends where it meets the brow area.

Line 1: the start

A line from the nostril straight up the face. The point where it meets the brow is where the brow should start. It usually passes close to the inner corner of the eye, but faces differ, so treat the eye corner as a check and not as the rule.

Line 2: the arch

A line from the nostril through the outer edge of the iris. The high point of the arch sits near where this line meets the brow.

Line 3: the end

A line from the nostril through the outer corner of the eye. The tail of the brow ends where this line meets the brow, at the same height as the start.

The level

The start and the end sit at the same height, measured along the face and not along the edge of the photo. If the head is tilted, a level line on the photo is not level on the face, so the tool measures along the line between the two pupils.

How to map a brow on a photo, step by step

These are the steps in the Skinstencil brow mapping tool, in order.

  1. Take a straight-on photo. Head level, camera at eye height, neutral face, eyes open and looking at the camera. Hair and makeup off the brows, even light.
  2. Confirm the client agreed and load the photo. Tick the consent box, then drop the photo in. The face is found on your device and nothing is uploaded.
  3. Check the face points. The tool places the pupils, the nostril points, the outer edge of each iris and the corners of the eyes. Drag any point that is off. If no face is found, place the points by hand.
  4. Set the scale. Enter the client's pupil to pupil distance in millimetres, or place a standard card next to the face and mark its long edge.
  5. Read start, arch and end. The three lines are drawn for both brows with the markers S, A and E. The thin outline is the client's existing brow from the face landmarks.
  6. Adjust the markers. Drag S, A and E where you want them. The lines, the level line and the measurements update at once.
  7. Download or print. Save the guides as a PNG, with or without the photo, or print them at true size with the ruler bar to check the scale.

Check the level and the symmetry

Once the markers are set, look at three things. First, the start and the end of each brow should be level, and the tool shows any difference in millimetres. Second, compare the two brows: width, length and arch height are listed side by side. Third, look at the distance between the two starts, which should suit the width of the nose and the space between the eyes.

Faces are rarely symmetrical, so small differences are normal. The numbers help you see them. What to do about them is your decision.

What a photo can and cannot tell you

A photo is flat. A head turned a little, a tilted chin or a wide-angle lens close to the face all change the distances. The tool warns when the eye line is tilted by more than 5 degrees or the face looks turned, but it cannot remove the effect. For the most reliable result, use a straight-on photo and check the guides on the client before you start.

Questions

Where does a brow start?

In classic brow mapping the start is where a line from the side of the nostril, drawn straight up the face, meets the brow. It often passes near the inner corner of the eye, but it is a guide and the artist decides.

How do you find the arch?

Draw a line from the nostril through the outer edge of the iris. The high point of the arch sits near where that line meets the brow.

Does the tool decide the brow shape?

No. It draws the standard guide lines and the measurements. Every point can be dragged, and the artist decides the final shape.

Is the client photo uploaded?

No. The face is found by a model that runs in your browser and is served from the same site. The photo is not sent anywhere and is not stored.
