For a reliable photo

  • Photograph straight on, with the head level and the camera at eye height.
  • Neutral face, eyes open, looking at the camera, brows relaxed.
  • Hair and makeup off the brows. Even light, no strong shadow.
  • Measure the client's pupil distance with a ruler and enter it, or place a card as a reference.

What brow mapping is

Brow mapping means marking where a brow should start, peak and end before any work begins. The classic method uses three lines drawn from the side of the nose: straight up for the start, through the outer edge of the iris for the arch, and through the outer corner of the eye for the end.

This tool draws those lines on a client photo, lets you drag every point, and reads off the distances in millimetres so you can print the guide at true size. It is a drawing aid. It does not decide the shape and it does not replace your judgement.

The lines this tool draws

  • Start: a line from the nostril straight up the face, level with the face and not with the photo edge. The brow starts where it meets the brow area.
  • Arch: a line from the nostril through the outer edge of the iris. The high point of the arch sits near where it meets the brow.
  • End: a line from the nostril through the outer corner of the eye. The tail ends where it reaches the level of the start.
  • Level: the start and the end sit at the same height. The tool shows any difference in millimetres.

What it cannot do

  • A photo is flat. Lens distortion, a slightly turned head or a tilted chin change the numbers.
  • The scale comes from the pupil distance you enter or the card you place. A wrong value scales everything wrongly.
  • It makes no medical or cosmetic claims and does not predict results. The artist decides the final shape.

Questions

Is my client's photo uploaded?

No. The face is found by a model that runs in your browser and is served from this site. The photo is not sent anywhere and nothing is stored. Checking a paid license key is the only request that leaves the page, and it never includes the photo.

How accurate are the measurements?

As accurate as the photo and the scale you give. A straight-on photo and a measured pupil distance work best. The tool has not yet been validated against measurements on real clients, so treat the numbers as a drawing aid and check them on the client.

Which lines does brow mapping use?

Three lines from the side of the nose: straight up for the start, through the outer edge of the iris for the arch, and through the outer corner of the eye for the end. The start and the end sit at the same height.

Can I print the guide at true size?

Yes. Enter the pupil distance or place a card, then print. The page prints at its width in millimetres and includes a ruler bar, so you can check the scale with a real ruler.

Is brow mapping free?
